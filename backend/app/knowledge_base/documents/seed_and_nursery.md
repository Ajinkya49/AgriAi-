# Seed Selection, Treatment and Nursery Management

Source: ICAR crop protection advisories; TNAU Agritech Portal. Compiled for Agri
AI's farming assistant.

Several of the most damaging crop diseases start at sowing. Bacterial spot in
pepper, late blight in potato, and damping off in every nursery are all far
cheaper to prevent at the seed stage than to treat later. A clean start is the
highest-return habit in the whole season.

## Choosing seed

- **Use certified seed** from a recognised source. It costs more per kilogram and
  saves multiples of that in reduced disease pressure.
- **Check the label** for germination percentage, purity and the date of testing.
  Old seed germinates poorly and produces weak seedlings.
- **Choose a variety suited to your district**, not just to the market. Ask your
  KVK which varieties are recommended locally, and prefer varieties with
  resistance to the diseases common in your area.
- **Do not save seed from a diseased crop.** For potato, never save tubers from a
  field that had bacterial wilt or late blight.

## Seed treatment

Seed treatment is cheap, quick, and one of the most effective disease measures
available.

**Hot water treatment** (for seed-borne bacteria such as *Xanthomonas* in pepper
and tomato):
1. Heat water to **50°C** and hold it there. Use a thermometer — this does not
   work by guesswork.
2. Soak the seed for **25 minutes**.
3. Cool immediately in cold water.
4. Dry in the **shade**, not in direct sun.
5. Sow as soon as possible.

Too hot kills the seed; too cool does not kill the bacteria. This is the one step
where precision genuinely matters.

**Fungicide or biocontrol treatment:**
- ***Trichoderma viride*** — mix 5-10 g per kg of seed with a sticker (gum arabic
  or a 2% starch solution) and sow immediately.
- ***Pseudomonas fluorescens*** — 10 g per kg of seed, similarly applied.
- Do not mix biocontrol agents with chemical fungicides; the chemical kills the
  organism you just applied.

**Traditional seed treatments (TNAU):**
- **Panchagavya** — soak seed or dip seedlings in a 3% solution for 20 minutes.
- **Amirthakaraisal** — ready the next day after mixing, used for seed treatment.
- **Sweet flag extract** — soak seed in 500 g powdered rhizome per 2.5 litres of
  water for 30 minutes, then dry in the shade.

## Nursery management

Damping off is the classic nursery failure: seedlings collapse at the soil line,
often in patches, and the whole bed can be lost in days.

**Preventing it:**
- Use a **clean, well-drained** potting mix. Never reuse mix from a bed that had
  damping off.
- Treat the nursery bed with ***Trichoderma viride*** at 10-15 g per kg of potting
  mix, or apply 250 g per square metre mixed into the top 5-8 cm.
- **Do not overwater.** A saturated nursery is where damping off begins.
- **Sow thinly.** Crowded seedlings stay wet and are easily infected from one
  another.
- **Give the nursery airflow.** Good ventilation is as important as any
  treatment, and it is free.
- **Raise the beds** so water drains away rather than pooling.

**Raising healthy seedlings:**
- Use a raised bed or trays with a free-draining medium.
- Water with a fine rose or drip — a strong stream damages young stems.
- Harden the seedlings before transplanting: reduce watering and expose them to
  more sun for 5-7 days beforehand.
- Transplant at the right age. Overgrown seedlings establish poorly and are more
  prone to stem disease.

## Transplanting

- **Dip the root ball** in a *Pseudomonas fluorescens* or *Trichoderma* slurry
  (10 g per litre) for 15-20 minutes before planting.
- **Do not bury the stem deeper than it was in the nursery.** Deep planting is a
  common cause of collar rot.
- **Water immediately after transplanting** to settle the soil around the roots.
- **Avoid transplanting in the heat of the day.** Late afternoon or an overcast
  day gives the seedling time to recover.

## Storage of seed and planting material

- Store seed cool, dry and dark, in a sealed container with a desiccant if
  available.
- Keep seed tubers of potato in a dark, ventilated place; light makes them green
  and unpalatable.
- Never store seed from a diseased crop with clean seed.

## Practical summary

1. Buy certified seed, or treat your own properly.
2. Hot water treatment at exactly 50°C for 25 minutes for pepper and tomato seed.
3. Treat with *Trichoderma* or *Pseudomonas* before sowing.
4. Keep the nursery clean, drained, thinly sown and ventilated.
5. Dip roots before transplanting and water in immediately.
6. Never save seed from a diseased crop.
