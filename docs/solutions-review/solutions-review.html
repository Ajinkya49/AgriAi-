Late blight is the most destructive potato disease in India. Water-soaked grey-green lesions spread into brown necrotic patches and white mould appears on leaf undersides in damp weather. Uproot and destroy affected plants immediately and keep them away from the field.

Tomato - Early Blight 7 entries

natural traced to source

Apply Trichoderma viride to the soil

Drench the root zone with Trichoderma viride at 5 g per litre of water, about 100-200 ml per plant, or mix 5 g per kg into the soil at transplanting. Trichoderma colonises the root surface, competes with the pathogen and produces enzymes that break down its cell walls. Use it as a preventive and early-season measure rather than a rescue treatment.

Source: TNAU Agritech Portal

Mulch and stake so soil does not splash onto the leaves

The early blight fungus overwinters in the soil and reaches the lower leaves on splashing water. Spread straw or plastic mulch around the base, stake the plants, and water at the base rather than overhead. Removing the route from soil to leaf breaks the cycle more reliably than treating the leaf afterwards.

Remove and destroy the infected lower leaves

Early blight always starts on the oldest, lowest leaves. Pick off affected leaves as soon as the brown concentric rings appear, put them in a bag, and take them out of the field. Do not compost them and do not leave them on the soil — the fungus survives in infected debris and re-infects the crop.

Pseudomonas fluorescens is a biocontrol bacterium widely used in India. Spray on the foliage at 5 g per litre of water, covering both leaf surfaces, in the evening. natural traced to source

Improve ventilation and lower the humidity

Leaf mold is a humidity disease — it barely appears in open fields but thrives in still, damp air. Prune lower leaves, increase plant spacing, open vents or side curtains, and water at the base only. Fixing the airflow usually does more than any spray.

Source: ICAR
