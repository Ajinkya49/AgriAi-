# Traditional Organic Preparations — Methods and Application

Source: TNAU Agritech Portal (Tamil Nadu Agricultural University), Organic Farming
section. Compiled for Agri AI's farming assistant.

These are long-standing preparations used in Indian organic farming. They are
recorded here because farmers ask about them and because they are documented by an
agricultural university — not because they are a substitute for identifying and
treating a specific disease.

**Important:** these are traditional practices. They support plant vigour and soil
biological activity. They are not a guaranteed treatment for an established
disease, and they should not be relied on alone when a crop is seriously affected.

## Panchagavya

Panchagavya is a fermented preparation of five cow-derived products plus a few
additions. TNAU describes it as promoting growth and supporting the plant's own
defences.

**Ingredients and method (TNAU).**

1. Mix **7 kg cow dung** with **1 kg cow ghee** thoroughly, morning and evening,
   and keep for **3 days**.
2. After 3 days, add **10 litres cow urine** and **10 litres water**, and keep
   for **15 days**, stirring morning and evening.
3. Then add **3 litres cow milk**, **2 litres cow curd**, **3 litres tender
   coconut water**, **3 kg jaggery** and **12 well-ripened poovan bananas**.
4. The stock solution is ready **30 days** after starting.

Keep the container open but in the shade, stir twice daily, and cover the mouth
with wire mesh or a mosquito net to keep flies out and prevent maggots. Use a
wooden stick for stirring. Do not mix buffalo products.

**Application (TNAU).**

- **Foliar spray:** 3 litres of stock solution per 100 litres of water (3%). This
  was found more effective than higher or lower concentrations.
- **With irrigation:** 50 litres per hectare through drip or flow irrigation.
- **Seed or seedling treatment:** soak seed or dip seedlings in a 3% solution for
  20 minutes. For turmeric, ginger and sugarcane setts, soak for 30 minutes.
- **Timing for tomato (TNAU):** nursery stage and 40 days after transplanting,
  with seed treatment at 1% for 12 hours.
- **General periodicity:** once in 15 days before flowering; once in 10 days
  during flowering and pod setting.

Filter the solution before spraying through a power sprayer, and use a nozzle
with a wider pore for hand-operated sprayers.

## Jeevamrut (Jivamirtham)

A fermented liquid used to build soil microbial activity.

**Ingredients (TNAU).** 10 kg cow dung, 10 litres cow urine, 2 kg old jaggery,
2 kg flour of gram / pigeon pea / moong / cowpea / urad dal, 1 kg live soil, and
200 litres of water.

**Method.** Take 100 litres of water in a barrel. Add the cow dung and cow urine
and mix well with a wooden stick. Add the jaggery and the flour and mix again.
Keep undisturbed for **2 to 7 days** to ferment, stirring regularly **three times
a day**.

**Use.** Apply around the root zone with irrigation. Farmers use it to support
soil biological activity and plant vigour.

## Amirthakaraisal

**Ingredients (TNAU).** 10 kg fresh cow dung, 10 litres cow urine, 1 kg country
jaggery, 100 litres water.

**Method.** Combine all ingredients in a cement tank and mix well. The extract can
be used **the next day** for seed treatment.

## Amrit Pani

**Method.** Mix 10 kg cow dung with 500 g honey into a creamy paste. Add 250 g
ghee and mix at high speed. Dilute with 200 litres of water.

## Neem preparations

Neem is the most widely used botanical in Indian organic farming, and it appears
in two distinct forms that should not be confused with each other.

**Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE).** Grind neem seed kernels, soak overnight in
water, strain, and spray. Used against a range of sucking and chewing pests.

**Neem oil spray.** Mix 3 ml neem oil per litre of water with a few drops of mild
soap to help it spread, and spray both leaf surfaces in the early morning or
evening.

**Neem leaf extract.** Soak 1 kg of fresh neem leaves in 10 litres of water
overnight, strain, and spray the filtrate. Published trials show it suppresses
*Alternaria solani* and other fungal pathogens, with higher concentrations giving
better control.

Neem products have documented antifungal and insecticidal activity. They work best
preventively and repeatedly, not as a single rescue spray.

## Other documented preparations

- **Prosopis decoction (Prosophis Kashayam).** Boil 2 kg of chopped Prosopis
  plant material (excluding roots) in 8 litres of water, reducing to one quarter
  of the volume. Cool, filter through cloth, and store. Yield about 2 litres.
- **Sweet flag extract.** Mix 500 g powdered sweet flag rhizome in 2.5 litres of
  water. Tie seed in small cloth bags and soak for 30 minutes, then dry in the
  shade before sowing.
- **Cow urine spray.** Dilute cow urine 1:10 with water and spray. Used as part
  of a wider programme rather than on its own.
- **Wood ash.** Dusted thinly over foliage in the early morning while dew is
  present, to dry the leaf surface and slow the spread of fungal disease.

## Honest limits

These preparations support plant health and soil biology. They are not a cure for
an established outbreak, and they are not a substitute for correct identification
of the problem. If a crop is seriously affected, act on the specific disease —
cultural measures and biocontrol first, and confirm with your KVK before relying
on any single remedy.

Where a preparation is described here as traditional, it means it is documented
practice. It does not mean it has been proven to control a named disease under
field conditions in every region.
